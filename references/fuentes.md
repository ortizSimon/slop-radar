# Finding good references for any piece

A starting point, not a menu. People ask AI for a thousand kinds of pieces; use the method first, then the bank. All links below were checked online in November 2026. Most sites search best in English.

## The method

**1. Name the profession, then find its three places.** Ask: who gets paid to make this? Every profession keeps its good work in three places:
- where it **publishes**: trade press and studio portfolios (Dieline for packaging, Motionographer for motion, LBB for advertising, BP&O for branding);
- where it **collects**: libraries, museums, archives, fan databases (NYPL menus, Design Reviewed, Discogs, Are.na);
- where it **judges**: juried awards (Pentawards, Clio, One Show, D&AD, TDC, SPD, World Illustration Awards, Caldecott, Bologna Ragazzi, Core77, Shorty, FWA, El Sol, El Ojo).

**2. Find the neighboring field that solved the same problem.** Describe the problem without the medium, then ask which other trade solves it every day:
- "a lot of information, readable at a glance" (menu, CV) → transit timetables, infographics, newspaper tables, type specimens;
- "keep them swiping" (carousel) → comics, storyboards, App Store screenshot sequences;
- "introduce yourself on one page" (CV, bio) → a magazine masthead, album liner notes, a book's back cover;
- "announce something that will happen" (invitation, launch) → posters, tickets, fashion show invitations, film title sequences;
- "a short sound people recognize" (audio logo) → interface sounds, jingles, radio idents;
- "show a product on the first screen" (landing page) → magazine covers, posters, shop windows.

**3. Look back in time.** The AI default is the average of recent work; old, well-made work sits outside it. Archives by decade (Design Reviewed, Web Design Museum), libraries and museums (NYPL Digital Collections, Rijksmuseum, V&A, Public Domain Review, Flickr Commons, Duke Ad*Access for old ads), and local archives (in Colombia, the Banco de la República collections).

**4. Judge the source.**
- Someone chooses and signs: a named curator or jury with a note per piece, not an algorithmic feed. Check the About page.
- Real and shipped: a live site, an app in the store, an ad that ran, with a date. A Dribbble or Behance concept never had to work.
- Context and reasons: credits, jury notes, case breakdowns, reviews. To tell the AI what to take, you need words.
- Depth and useful filters: years of archive and filters by what changes the piece (color, industry, decade, film stock, country). "Trending" is not a filter; it is the average.
- Commercial bias: sponsored entries, studios featuring their own work, galleries selling templates of the look they show. Weigh them less.
- **The default of references:** the same example on every list, the same fonts, bento grids, gradients and glass, AI-generated images (warped text, perfect symmetry), template mills (Canva, Freepik, Envato, CV generators). When you see it, go one level down: follow the credit to the studio, and from the studio to its own references.

**5. Safety check before recommending a site.** HTTPS and an operator you can name (an institution, museum, company, award or named editor). No download needed just to look, and downloads only from known sources with clear licenses. No piracy (full films, "free" paid fonts, brand manuals uploaded by third parties, "download the book PDF"). No ad walls, pop-ups or redirect chains. If it needs a login or a card, say so before sending someone. Prefer the primary source (the brand, the museum, the award) over a blog that copies its images.

**6. Ready-to-use searches** (replace what is in `< >`):
- `"<field or thing> award winners <year>"` · `"<award>" shortlist`
- `"<thing> design" site:are.na` · `site:are.na "<thing>"`
- `"<company>" design system` · `"<company>" brand guidelines` · `"<company>" press kit` or `newsroom`
- `"<thing>" case study` · `"<brand>" identity process`
- `"<thing>" archive` · `"<thing>" collection museum` · `vintage "<thing>" library` · `<thing> 1960s`
- `"<thing>" inspiration curated -template -free -generator` (removes template mills)
- `site:fontsinuse.com "<thing>"` (real printed and digital pieces, with credits)
- `"<profession>" studio portfolio <city>`
- Ads that really ran: the Meta, TikTok, Google and LinkedIn ad libraries.
- Product videos on X: `filter:native_video min_faves:500 "introducing"` (unofficial operators, may change).

**7. Save the why.** For each reference: the link, what to take ("only the palette", "how the price sits on top", "the rhythm of the cuts") and what not to take. Two or three per piece, from different fields. The most personal reference is usually the user's own: brands they love, a place, their past work, their own data. Libraries made "for AI" become the next average the more they are used.

## The bank

Format: **Name**: link to the right section · what it is · *Search:* what to do there · *Watch out:* catches.

### Websites and landing pages
- **Recent** (formerly Godly): https://recent.design/websites · hand-picked current sites. *Search:* tap your industry tag, open ten, compare first screens. *Watch out:* sponsors.
- **Land-book**: https://land-book.com/design/landing-page (also `/design/pricing-page`, `/sections`, `/motion`) · *Search:* sort by featured, open your page type, compare hero hierarchy. *Watch out:* downloads are Pro, some ads.
- **MaxiBestOf**: https://maxibestof.one/sections · sections of real sites with the font each uses (pairings at `/typefaces`).
- **Curated**: https://www.curated.design · live sites reviewed one by one, filters by style and attributes. *Watch out:* sponsored entries; advanced filters paid.
- **Siteinspire**: https://www.siteinspire.com · classic curated gallery, by style and type.
- **Web Design Museum**: https://www.webdesignmuseum.org/gallery (by year) and `/exhibitions` (by theme) · how your industry looked around 2000; a way out of the average.

### App and product UI (onboarding, motion)
- **Mobbin**: iOS https://mobbin.com/discover/apps/ios/latest · Web https://mobbin.com/discover/apps/web/latest · Sites https://mobbin.com/discover/sites/latest · screens and flows from shipped apps. *Search:* an app in your category, open its screens or flows. *Watch out:* login; free tier limited.
- **Page Flows**: https://pageflows.com/web/flows/onboarding/ (and `/ios/flows/onboarding/`) · annotated recordings of real flows. *Search:* filter by app type, watch the whole video. *Watch out:* paid, short trial.
- **60fps**: https://60fps.design/apps (by category, e.g. `/apps/category/finance`) · animations and interactions from real apps. *Search:* how long and how each transition eases.
- **Growth.Design**: https://growth.design/case-studies · free case studies with the psychology behind each decision.
- **Design Spells**: https://www.designspells.com · micro-interactions from shipped apps, each explained.
- **Refero**: https://refero.design · web and iOS screens searchable in plain language ("pricing page with a toggle"). *Watch out:* paid.

### Logos and identity
- **Brand New**: reviews at https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/archives/category/editorial/reviewed/ · by industry, e.g. `/archives/category/industry/food` · real rebrands with long reviews. *Search:* your industry; read the review for the why.
- **Logobook**: https://logobook.com/shapes/ · by industry https://logobook.com/business/finance/ · by letter https://logobook.com/letter/s/ · classic logos with designer and year.
- **BP&O**: https://bpando.org/logos/ · reviews with reasoning.
- **Official brand sites**: e.g. https://brand.dropbox.com · https://linear.app/brand · https://www.ibm.com/design/language · search `"<brand>" brand guidelines`.

### Type
- **Fonts In Use**: by format, e.g. https://fontsinuse.com/in/2/formats/9/branding-identity · `/in/2/formats/3/web` · real pieces with the fonts identified.
- **Typewolf**: https://www.typewolf.com/site-of-the-day · https://www.typewolf.com/not-on-google-fonts (out of the default).
- **Future Fonts**: https://www.futurefonts.com/typefaces · independent fonts almost nobody uses yet. *Watch out:* paid licenses.
- *Note:* Satoshi, General Sans and Clash (Fontshare) are already the default of indie landing pages.

### Posters, editorial and print
- **Design Reviewed**: https://designreviewed.com/archive/ · by decade, e.g. `/1960s-graphic-design-history/` · by country, e.g. `/country/japan/` · 11,000+ scanned pieces, archive free.
- **typo/graphic posters**: https://www.typographicposters.com/posters · 12,000+ posters from 45 countries.
- **100 Beste Plakate**: https://100-beste-plakate.de/plakate/filter/jahr:2025/ · the year's 100 best posters, picked by a jury.
- **eMuseum (Museum für Gestaltung Zürich)**: https://www.emuseum.ch · the Swiss poster collection.
- **Coverjunkie**: https://www.coverjunkie.com · magazine covers.

### Photography and image prompts
- **FILMGRAB**: https://film-grab.com/movies-a-z/ · search e.g. `/?s=neon` · stills from thousands of films. *Search:* pick two or three frames for the light. *Watch out:* ads.
- **ShotDeck**: https://shotdeck.com · licensed stills with filters by color, shot size and light. *Watch out:* paid, with trial.
- **Frame Set**: https://frameset.app/search · search with a full sentence, like a prompt.
- **Magnum Photos**: https://www.magnumphotos.com/photographers/ · take the photographer's framing and light, not the photo.
- **Are.na**: *Img: Product Lighting* https://www.are.na/lu-nty5h7yjoyq/img-product-lighting · *Colors / Colorgrade / Lighting* https://www.are.na/current-that-flows/colors-colorgrade-lighting

### 3D and product

- **Core77 Design Awards**: https://designawards.core77.com/Consumer-Technology · juried products.

### Problems, plans and advice
The strongest anchor here is the user's own situation: their numbers, constraints, customers and what they already tried. Then real cases from people who solved something similar.
- **First Round Review**: https://review.firstround.com · long, practical pieces by operators on hiring, growth, pricing and management, with what they actually did.
- **Growth.Design**: https://growth.design/case-studies · product and growth decisions explained case by case.
- **Y Combinator Library**: https://www.ycombinator.com/library · essays and talks from founders on early growth and selling.
- **Failory › Cemetery**: https://www.failory.com/cemetery · postmortems of startups that closed, with why. What didn't work is as useful as what did.
- **Harvard Business Review**: https://hbr.org · research and cases on strategy and management. *Watch out:* partly paywalled.
- **Google Scholar**: https://scholar.google.com · studies on the question; search `"<problem>" field study` or `"<problem>" meta-analysis`.
- **Data**: Our World in Data https://ourworldindata.org for global numbers; for Colombia, DANE https://www.dane.gov.co. Real numbers beat assumptions in any plan.
- **People who solved it:** search `"<problem>" case study <industry>` or `"how we" <result> <industry>`, and look for the person or business, not the listicle.
